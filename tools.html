Spend your own money last

What I load on the truck, and the order I would buy it.

One page for a groundman or apprentice. Not a local tool list. Ask your crew before you treat this as required.

As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases. I may earn a commission if you buy through these links, at no extra cost to you.

Buy in this order — hand tools

  1. Klein D213-9NE, 9-inch side-cutting pliers. Daily cutter and grip. Buy these before any power tool.
  2. Channellock 430X, 10-inch tongue-and-groove pliers. Connectors, locks, and anything the nines will not hold.
  3. Klein D507-12, 12-inch adjustable wrench. Hardware that is not a socket job. One good one.
  4. Klein 44200 cable-splicer knife. URD jacket and rope. If it will not open with gloves, leave it.
  5. Headlamp plus spare batteries. Spare rides in the bag, not the truck.
  6. Klein NCVT1P pocket voltage tester. A pen, not a clearance. Prove it on a known live source before a cabinet.
  7. Marker, tape, and paint. Name the pliers the day they come out of the package.
  8. Boots for a 10-hour day. Cheap ones come off by lunch. Break them in off the job.
  9. Rain shell and a second light. The first light gets buried. The second one is the one you find.

Pad-mount tools, after the hand tools

  1. Rauckman 13/16 penta socket, 1/2-inch drive. Most cabinet doors use a penta head. A regular socket will not turn it.
  2. Klein Tools 66090 1/2-Inch Impact Socket Set, 2-in-1 Slotted Design. Six sockets, twelve sizes, in a case. The 1-1/8 x 15/16 and 3/4 x 9/16 have the alignment slot. Half-inch drive. This is the only socket set. A mechanic set does not replace it.
  3. 1/2, 3/8, and 1/4 ratchets. Half-inch runs the 2-in-1 when the impact will not fit. Smaller drives for cabinet hardware.
  4. Spare penta and a door pin. A rounded penta or a missing pin ends the job.
  5. Padlock and hasp hardware. Company lock stays on the company ring. Yours is the spare, marked.
  6. Insulated nut driver. Secondary compartment hardware. Hand tool, not the impact.
  7. Wire brush. Ground pads and lugs that will not take a connection until they are clean.
  8. Elbow puller, only if issued. Do not buy one until the contractor tells you it is yours to carry.

Leave these at the store

  • A mechanic socket set. The Klein 2-in-1 is the set. Extra sockets sit in the truck.
  • Cheap lockback that needs two hands. Gloves make it a pocket weight.
  • Second impact before the hand tools are marked. The wrench does not replace pliers or a penta socket.
  • Your own grounding set or live-line tools. Those come from the contractor. Do not freelance them.

Before the first call

Boots, glasses, gloves, knife, tape, headlamp. On pad-mount: penta socket, Klein 2-in-1, 1/2, 3/8, and 1/4 ratchets, spare penta, marked lock.
