Getting help with drug use

Long shifts, time away from home, pain, and pressure can make asking for help feel difficult. You do not need to wait for things to get worse.

  1. Talk with a qualified healthcare professional. Tell them what you take and how often, including prescriptions and alcohol. Ask what a safe next step looks like. Do not stop or change medication based on this page.
  2. Find treatment that fits your situation. Ask about an assessment, treatment options, medication when appropriate, costs, and appointments that work around your schedule.
  3. Bring in one trusted person. A friend, family member, or supportive crewmate can help you make the call or get to an appointment. You can also ask your union or employee assistance program what support is available and what its confidentiality limits are.

SAMHSA's National Helpline offers free, confidential treatment referrals and information, 24/7, in English and Spanish. Treatment provider fees vary. No coaching purchase is needed to use these resources.

Safety comes first

If someone cannot be awakened or is breathing slowly or not at all, call 911 immediately. For a suspected opioid overdose, give naloxone if available and follow the product and emergency dispatcher's instructions.

Do not climb, drive, operate equipment, or work energized lines while impaired or unable to work safely. Seek medical guidance about withdrawal rather than trying to push through a shift.

For a mental health or substance use crisis in the U.S., call or text 988 or visit 988 Lifeline. This website and coaching email are not emergency services.

Life coaching for linemen

My goal is to build a life-coaching practice for fellow linemen: a place to talk about where you want to go and turn that into practical next steps. The focus is everyday goals, healthier routines, work-life balance, and accountability—not diagnosing or treating addiction.

No coaching certification or clinical license is claimed here. Coaching is not therapy, medical care, addiction treatment, or a substitute for professional support. Recovery is not guaranteed, and coaching is not a condition of accessing the free resources above.
