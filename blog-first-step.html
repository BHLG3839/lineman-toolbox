Your first step toward addiction support—without figuring everything out today

October 17, 2026 · Lineman Tool Box

You can ask for help before you have a perfect plan. The first task is not to solve your whole future. It is to connect with someone qualified to help you work out what comes next.

General education for U.S. readers. Not medical advice, a detox plan, or a determination of fitness for duty. No clinical review is claimed.

1. Start with one honest sentence

If you work long shifts or travel for storm work, fitting in a conversation may feel like another job. Keep the opening simple:

“I'm concerned about my drug or alcohol use, and I want help figuring out a safe next step.”

You can say that to a healthcare professional or a treatment intake team. Tell the clinician what substances and medications you take and how often. You do not need to diagnose yourself first, and this article cannot tell you which treatment you need.

2. Use a treatment referral—not a sales pitch

SAMHSA's National Helpline is a free, confidential, 24/7 information and treatment-referral service in English and Spanish. Call 1-800-662-HELP (4357). It connects people with local resources; it does not provide counseling.

You can also search FindTreatment.gov for treatment providers. The referral service being free does not mean every treatment program is free. Ask providers about insurance, self-pay prices, and financial assistance before committing.

3. Bring questions that fit your working life

These are conversation starters—not promises about what any provider or employer will offer:

  • What assessment do I need, and how soon can I be seen?
  • What treatment options might be appropriate after an assessment?
  • Can appointments work around travel or changing shifts?
  • What will this cost, and do you accept my insurance?
  • What information stays confidential, and what are the exceptions?
  • Who can help me understand any work restrictions and the process for returning safely?

If you have a union support contact or employee assistance program, ask what help is available and what its privacy rules are. Do not assume that asking for assistance guarantees confidentiality, job protection, or a particular return-to-work outcome.

4. Put immediate safety ahead of the shift

Do not climb, drive, operate equipment, or work energized lines while impaired or unable to work safely. Follow applicable workplace safety procedures and seek qualified guidance. Do not use a blog post to decide that you are fit for duty.

Ask a healthcare professional how to make changes safely. Do not stop or change prescribed medications based on this article, and do not try to push through withdrawal at work.

Someone who cannot be awakened or whose breathing is slow or has stopped needs emergency attention. SAMHSA advises calling 911 immediately for signs of opioid overdose. Give naloxone if available and follow its instructions and the emergency dispatcher's directions.

5. Choose one next step today

Pick one manageable action: call the referral line, contact a provider, or ask someone you trust to sit with you while you make the call. Write down the next appointment or callback time so it does not get lost between shifts.

Life coaching can focus on everyday goals and accountability, but it is not addiction treatment, detox, or therapy. You never need to purchase coaching to use the resources in this article.
