02 / Your crew

Show up.
Listen first.

You don’t have to be a counselor to care about someone. A private, honest check-in can be a place to start.

Open the conversation.

Choose a safe moment away from the work and the crowd. Share what you’ve noticed without diagnosing or making assumptions.

“You haven’t seemed like yourself lately. How are you doing?”
“I’m here to listen. What would feel helpful right now?”

Let them talk.

Listen without judgment. Avoid telling them to toughen up, comparing struggles, or promising you can fix everything. Ask whether they’d like help finding professional support.

If you’re concerned about suicide.

Ask directly: “Are you thinking about suicide?” Take their answer seriously. You can call or text 988 in the U.S. for guidance when you’re worried about someone else.

If someone is in immediate physical danger or a suicide attempt is underway, call 911 in the U.S. Keep your own safety in mind and follow emergency responders’ instructions.

Follow up without carrying it alone.

Check back in. Offer to help them make a call or find a clinician. Respect their privacy, but don’t promise secrecy when someone’s safety is at risk. You deserve support, too.
