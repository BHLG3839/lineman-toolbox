Whether it’s a hard day or a crisis, there’s a next step. These free support helplines are for the United States.

Need support right now?

Call or text 988 for free, confidential support. For mental-health, suicide, or substance-use crisis support, 24/7 in the U.S. You can also contact 988 if you’re worried about someone else.

Call 988Text 988Online support ↗

Immediate physical danger or medical emergency? Call 911. Outside the U.S., contact your local emergency number or crisis service.

Free mental-health & addiction helplines

SAMHSA National Helpline

1-800-662-HELP (4357) — Free, confidential treatment referral and information for mental-health and alcohol or drug-use concerns. Available 24/7 in English and Spanish for individuals and families. This is a referral service, not counseling; referred treatment may have costs.

Call 1-800-662-4357

Disaster Distress Helpline

1-800-985-5990 — Free, confidential crisis counseling for emotional distress related to natural or human-caused disasters. Available 24/7.

Call 1-800-985-5990Text the helpline

Phone services are free; your carrier’s standard messaging or data rates may apply. Helpline details from SAMHSA.

Looking for ongoing care?

A primary-care clinician or licensed mental-health professional can help you explore care. If you have insurance, ask your plan about covered providers and costs.

If your employer or union offers an Employee Assistance Program (EAP), ask how to access it, what it covers, and what confidentiality limits apply. Availability and terms vary.

Not sure what to say?

“I’m struggling and would like to talk with someone.”
“Can you help me understand my options and what this will cost?”

About this toolbox

Lineman Toolbox is being rebuilt around mental health for lineworkers and their families. This site provides general information—not diagnosis, treatment, or a monitored crisis service. This page does not collect messages or personal health information. The separate recovery-story signup page is for email updates, not urgent help.
